Sewer Camera Inspection in Rocky Hill, CT

A waterproof camera on a flexible cable shows what is inside the sewer line, so repairs are based on the pipe and not on guesses.

A sewer camera inspection sends a small camera through your drain line while a monitor shows the pipe wall live. It answers the questions a cable cannot: what is blocking the line, where the damage is and how bad it looks. Book it for repeat clogs, a home purchase or a suspected break.

This is a planned service, but our 24/7 line also takes urgent calls. If sewage is already backing up, call (860) 397-8103 now and the plumber can run the camera once the line is open.

What a sewer camera inspection is

The plumber feeds a camera head on a push cable into the line through a cleanout, or through a pulled toilet if there is no cleanout. The head carries lights, and the picture is shown on a monitor as it travels. The cable has distance markings, so each finding has a position along the pipe.

Many cameras also carry a transmitter. A locator held at the surface picks up its signal and shows where the camera sits and how deep. That turns a finding on video into a spot on the ground.

When a camera is worth calling for

Repeat clogs

A line that blocks every few months has a reason. The camera shows whether it is roots, grease, a sag or a break.

Buying a house

Sewer lines are out of sight. A video of the main line before closing shows its condition before you own it.

Suspected leak or break

Sewer odor in the yard, a soft patch of lawn or a cable that stops at one spot are reasons to look inside.

Planning a repair

Before any digging or lining, the camera sets the length and type of the work so the price is based on facts.

Pre-purchase inspections

A home inspection rarely covers the buried sewer line. A camera inspection before you close shows if the line is sound or if you would inherit root intrusion or a collapsed section. Ask for a copy of the video.

In Rocky Hill, much of the housing stock was built in the 1960s through the 1980s, based on town census counts. Pipe that old is where roots and cracked joints show up, so a pre-purchase look at an older house is a sensible use of a camera.

05

Explain the findings

You see the footage and hear what needs doing, from nothing at all to a repair.

Reading the findings

Not every flaw needs action. A slight offset in an old joint on a line that drains well may only need monitoring. A cracked section with root growth deserves a plan. A collapse needs repair soon. Ask the plumber to rank each finding as watch, plan or fix now.

If the camera shows damage, our sewer line repair page explains spot repair, lining and replacement. If it shows buildup or roots on a sound pipe, sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting is the next step.

Camera limits and Rocky Hill connections

A camera cannot show a blockage it cannot pass, and it cannot see through standing water in a belly. The plumber may need to clear the line first. A camera shows your side of the line as far as the camera reaches.

The Metropolitan District Commission (MDC) provides sewer service in Rocky Hill. If the footage shows trouble near the connection to the public main, ask MDC what falls on its side. Work in the street needs the town's Street Excavation Permit and a Call Before You Dig number.

What a camera inspection costs

The plumber gives a clear price before work starts. These factors affect it.

What moves the price

  • Access: a cleanout is quick, while pulling a toilet takes more time.
  • Length of the line and how many turns it has.
  • Whether the line must be cleared first so the camera can pass.
  • Locating: marking depth and position at the surface adds time.
  • A copy of the video for a sale, a claim or a second opinion.
Insurance note: a camera inspection is usually a diagnostic cost, not a covered repair. Many standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental damage but not gradual wear, and policies vary, so check yours.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you take urgent sewer calls at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. If sewage is backing up, say so and we book the first plumber who can reach you. After-hours visits are priced differently, and you get the price before work starts.

Does a camera inspection fix the problem?

No. It finds the cause and the location. Clearing or repairing the line is a separate step, though the plumber can often move on to it, with your approval and a clear price, once the footage shows what is needed.

Can I get a copy of the video?

Ask when you book. A recording is useful for a home sale, a second opinion or an insurance question. Tell the dispatcher you want it so the plumber sets up to record.

Should I get a camera inspection before buying a house?

It is a good idea, especially for an older home or one with mature trees near the sewer route. A routine home inspection does not cover the buried line, and repairs can be large.

How does the plumber know where the problem is?

The cable has distance markings and many cameras carry a transmitter. A locator at the surface picks up the signal, showing the position and depth of the camera, so the spot can be marked before any digging.
