What counts as a plumbing emergency

Call right away for water you cannot stop, sewage coming into the house, no water at all, a stopped sump pump with rising water, or a leaking water heater. These problems get worse by the hour and damage floors, walls and belongings.

A slow drain, a dripping faucet or a running toilet can usually wait for a daytime visit. If you are not sure which category yours falls in, call and ask. The dispatcher can tell you whether it can wait and what to do in the meantime.

While you wait for a plumber

  1. Shut off the water to the fixture, or the main shutoff if you cannot find the right valve.
  2. Move belongings away from the water, and stay clear of outlets and cords in wet areas.
  3. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency number from outside.
  4. Note what you see and hear so the dispatcher can send the right plumber with the right parts.

How to choose the right service

If you know the symptom but not the cause, pick the page that matches the symptom. A wet ceiling points to leak detection, while a drain that will not clear points to drain cleaning. The plumber confirms the cause on site and tells you if a different service fits better.

For repeat problems, ask for a camera look first. It costs less than guessing, and it shows whether you need a cleaning or a repair. Our sewer line repair page explains what comes next if the pipe is damaged.

Planned work versus urgent calls

Planned jobs like installs, upgrades and repiping can be scheduled for a time that suits you. Urgent calls go to the front of the queue. The same 24/7 line takes both, so you do not need a different number.

Before work starts, you get a clear price. Plumbers work from model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Connecticut's state building code is based on the 2021 International Codes, so before a planned install, ask your building department what applies to your job.
