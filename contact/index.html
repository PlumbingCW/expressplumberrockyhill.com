What to tell dispatch

A few details let us book the right plumber with the right equipment.

Have these ready when you call

  1. The address, including the unit or floor if you live in a condo or apartment.
  2. What you see: where the water is, how fast it is moving, and whether sewage is involved.
  3. Whether you found and closed the main shutoff or the fixture valve.
  4. Which fixture or appliance is affected, such as a water heater, toilet, sink or floor drain.
  5. Anything unsafe: a gas smell, water near outlets, or no way to reach the shutoff.
  6. A callback number in case the call drops, and the best door or entrance to use.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 first. Then call us once you are outside and safe.

When to call and when to email

Call for anything urgent

Active leaks, backups, no water, no hot water and anything unsafe go to the phone line. It is staffed 24/7, so you reach a person at any hour.

Email for non-urgent questions

A general question about services or coverage can go to the email address above. Email is not monitored like the phone line, so do not use it when water is moving.

Visiting the office

Our office is on Silas Deane Highway in Rocky Hill. Plumbers are dispatched to you, so call before you drive over, as the office is not a walk-in service counter.

Condos and rentals

Tenants should tell the landlord or building manager about a leak, since the shutoff may be in a shared space. Then call us with the unit number and the manager's contact if you have it.

What happens after you call

The dispatcher gives you safety steps first, then books the next suitable plumber. Urgent problems go ahead of routine ones. We offer same-day appointments when available.

When the plumber has seen the problem, you get a clear price before work starts. You can read more on how we work or browse the plumbing services we cover.
