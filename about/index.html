Licensed and insured

The work is done by licensed plumbers, and the company is licensed and insured. We think you should be able to check that for yourself.

Verify with the state

Connecticut plumbing licenses are issued by the Department of Consumer Protection. Before any plumber starts work, ask for the license class and look it up with the DCP licensing division.

Match the class to the job

A P-1 contractor license covers all plumbing and piping work. A limited license, such as a P-7 for water, sewer and storm lines, covers a narrower scope. Ask which one applies to your job.

How we price

Plumbing cost depends on what is wrong, how hard it is to reach, which parts are needed and what hour you call. A drain that needs a hand tool is a different job from one that needs a cable machine or a jetter.

That is why we do not quote a number to someone who has not been seen. The plumber diagnoses first, then gives you a clear price before work starts. If the job grows, you hear about it before it happens. After-hours visits are priced differently from weekday ones, and we tell you that up front.

How we scope work before it starts

Scoping means finding the cause before anyone picks up a wrench. The plumber asks what you noticed, checks the fixture, the shutoff and the lines around it, and tells you what they found in plain words.

Then you get the price. It covers the work described, and if something changes mid-job, such as a corroded valve behind a wall, the plumber stops and explains before going further. You never receive a surprise on the way out the door.

Cost factors are the same on every job: the cause, how hard the spot is to reach, the parts, whether the work is a repair or a replacement, and the hour of the call. Ask the plumber which of those applies to your job.

What licensed and insured means for you

Licensed means the plumber holds a Connecticut license issued by the Department of Consumer Protection. Insured means the company carries coverage if something goes wrong on a job. Both matter, because plumbing mistakes can damage a home or fail an inspection.

Connecticut sorts plumbing licenses into classes. A P-1 Unlimited Plumbing Contractor may do all plumbing and piping work. A P-2 journeyperson works under a licensed contractor. A P-7 limited contractor covers water, sewer and storm lines from the utility's responsibility point to just inside a structure.

Before work starts, ask the plumber for the license class and look it up with the DCP licensing division. A plumber who cannot answer that question is a red flag, whoever the company is.
