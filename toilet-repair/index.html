Most toilet problems come down to a few parts: the flapper, the fill valve, the wax seal, the flange or a clog in the trap. Knowing which one is failing decides whether the fix takes twenty minutes or an afternoon. This page helps you tell them apart, and shows what you can safely do before a plumber arrives.

What to do right now if the toilet is overflowing

Water on the bathroom floor is the urgent part. It moves under vinyl and into the ceiling below faster than most people expect. Stop the flow first, then deal with the cause.

If the water in the bowl is clear and comes from the tank, the problem is usually a stuck fill valve or flapper. If the water is dirty or smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets out of the room.

Call (860) 397-8103 once the water is stopped. Tell us whether it is still rising, whether other fixtures are slow, and whether the water is clean.

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Turn the shutoff valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  2. If the valve is stuck, lift the tank lid and raise the float or hold the flapper closed so the tank stops refilling.
  3. Do not flush again and do not plunge a bowl that is already full to the rim.
  4. Lay towels at the base and keep electrical cords and power strips off the wet floor.
  5. If sewage is on the floor, wear rubber gloves, avoid contact with skin and wash your hands well afterward.

A toilet that keeps running

A running toilet wastes water around the clock and rarely fixes itself. The usual culprit is a flapper that has hardened or warped, so it no longer seals the tank. Water then leaks into the bowl, and the fill valve opens to top it off again and again.

Some running toilets only cycle on at odd times, a sign of a slow flapper leak. A few drops of food coloring in the tank, left for fifteen minutes without flushing, will show color in the bowl if the flapper leaks. A worn fill valve or an overfill tube set too high can do the same thing, and a plumber replaces the whole set of parts in one visit when the toilet is older.

Leaks at the base, behind the tank or below the floor

Water around the base after a flush usually means the wax ring has failed, because the seal can no longer hold when the toilet shifts. Water behind the toilet is more often the supply line or the shutoff valve, and a leak from the tank bolts shows up as drips under the tank.

Do not ignore a slow leak at the floor. It soaks the subfloor, and in a crawl space or a ceiling below it can show up weeks later as a stain or a smell. If you find moisture under the house near the drain, tell us when you call. For hidden moisture with no obvious source, our leak detection service traces it.

Rocking toilets and flange problems

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to and that connects the bowl to the drain. When it cracks, rusts or sits below the finished floor, the toilet rocks and the seal breaks again and again.

Retightening bolts can help a toilet that has loosened a little. If the bolts keep slipping, or the flange itself has broken, a plumber repairs it with a new ring or a repair plate, then sets the toilet on a fresh seal. New tile can leave the flange too low, which needs an extension.

Clogs: what we try and when it goes deeper

A one-time clog

A flange plunger often clears a single blockage. Toys, wipes and excess paper are common. If a plunger does not clear it in a few tries, stop and call.

A toilet that clogs repeatedly

A low-flow bowl with a weak flush, a partial obstruction in the trap or a vent problem can all cause repeat clogs. We check the trap and the fixture first.

Several fixtures acting up

Gurgling, slow tubs and a toilet that bubbles point to the branch or main drain. Our drain cleaning service clears those lines.

Sewage rising in the lowest drain

If a basement floor drain also backs up, the main line is blocked. See sewer line cleaning for that job.

Repair or replace the toilet

Most toilets are worth repairing. A new flapper, fill valve, supply line or wax seal brings an older toilet back to normal for a small part of the cost of a new one.

Replacement makes sense when the bowl or tank is cracked, when porcelain is leaking from the inside, or when an older high-flow model clogs constantly and a better flush is the real fix. A new toilet also needs a sound flange and shutoff valve, so we check those when we pull the old one. For wider fixture changes, see plumbing installation.

What toilet repair costs

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that decide the number, so you know what to expect when you call.

What moves the price

  • What failed: a flapper or fill valve is a small repair, a broken flange or a cracked bowl is not.
  • Whether the toilet must be pulled and reset, which is needed for any seal or flange work.
  • Access to the shutoff valve, supply line and floor around the base.
  • Parts: standard repair kits cost less than special-fit or higher-end fixtures.
  • Time of the call: nights, weekends and holidays are usually priced differently from a weekday visit.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell us what the toilet is doing and we book the first plumber who can get to your address. After-hours visits are priced differently from weekday ones, and we give you the price before work starts.

How do I stop an overflowing toilet?

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed or raise the float so the tank stops refilling. Then stop flushing, mop up and call. Do not keep plunging a full bowl.

Why does my toilet run on its own every few minutes?

Usually a flapper that no longer seals lets tank water slip into the bowl, and the fill valve tops it off again. Food coloring in the tank will show color in the bowl if the flapper leaks. A worn fill valve can do the same thing.

Is it safe to use my toilet if it leaks at the base?

A small seep can wait a short time if you keep the area dry, but the leak soaks the subfloor with every flush. Stop using the toilet, close the shutoff valve and call. A damaged subfloor costs far more than the seal that caused it.

Should I repair or replace an old toilet?

Repair if the porcelain is sound and the problem is a flapper, fill valve, seal or supply line. Replace if the bowl or tank is cracked, or if it clogs constantly. The plumber checks the flange and shutoff valve before recommending either path.
