Repiping in Rocky Hill, CT

Repiping replaces worn supply pipe with new copper or PEX. We explain what needs to go, what can stay and how the work disrupts your days before you commit.

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes in your house, either all of them or the failing runs. It is a planned project, but our 24/7 line also takes urgent calls if a pipe fails before the work is scheduled. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

Most homeowners call because of a pattern: repeated leaks, rusty water, weak pressure or a pipe that just split. One repair is normal. Several in a few years usually means the rest of the pipe is near the end of its life.

Signs it may be time to repipe

  • Leaks keep appearing in different places, even after each one is fixed.
  • Water runs brown or orange after sitting overnight, or tastes metallic.
  • Pressure is weak at several fixtures although the main valve is fully open.
  • Visible pipe is flaking, green, white-crusted or stained at the joints.
  • The house still has galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside.
  • A plumber has already told you a section is too thin or too brittle to patch.

Pipe materials compared

Three materials cover nearly all residential supply repiping today. The right one depends on the house, the layout and your budget.

A general guide. The plumber recommends a material after seeing your layout and the pipe you have.
MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
CopperLong track record, handles heat, tolerates sun and tight code situationsHigher material and labor cost, soldered joints take time
PEXFlexible, fewer joints, quicker in finished walls, resists freeze splits better than rigid pipeMust be protected from sunlight and some chemicals, needs correct fittings
Galvanized steelCommon in older homesCorrodes inside, restricts flow, no longer a choice for new supply work

Whole house or partial repipe

A partial repipe replaces one failing section, such as a kitchen line, a bathroom stack or the runs in a crawl space. It makes sense when the rest tests sound and the problem is local. You spend less and the disruption is smaller.

A whole-house repipe replaces the supply system from the main shutoff to every fixture. It fits when pipe is galvanized, when leaks recur or when a remodel already has walls open. Doing it once also avoids patching one corroded run after another.

How a repipe goes, step by step

01

Walk-through and plan

The plumber looks at the main shutoff, the pipe runs, the access points and the fixtures, then lays out a route and gives a clear price before work starts.

02

Permit questions

Ask the Rocky Hill building department what applies to your job. Work follows the state building code, which is based on the 2021 International Codes.

03

Water off, old pipe out

The water is shut off for a planned window. Access holes are cut where needed and old pipe is removed or abandoned.

04

New pipe in

New lines are run to each fixture, tied in at the main and the water heater, and connected to shutoffs.

05

Pressure test and turn on

We pressurize the system, check every joint, flush the lines and restore water to the house.

06

Patch and handoff

Access holes are closed. Painting and finish repairs are agreed in advance and often handled by a drywall or paint trade.

What the disruption looks like

Time without water

Water is typically off for part of each day while connections are made, not for the whole project. The plumber plans the schedule with you.

Dust and access holes

Finished walls get small openings. We protect floors and keep openings as limited as the layout allows.

Duration

A small partial repipe may be a day. A full house takes longer. The plumber gives you a schedule with the price.

Staying in the house

Many people stay home with a plan for water. Others arrange a night away on the busiest day.

What repiping costs

We give a clear price before work starts. Repiping is a large job, so these factors matter most.

What moves the price

  • Number of fixtures and the size of the house.
  • Pipe material: copper, PEX or a mix.
  • Access: open basement or crawl space is simpler than finished walls and ceilings.
  • Whether drywall repair and painting are included or left to another trade.
  • Permits, inspections and whether the water heater and shutoff valves are replaced at the same time.
Insurance note: replacing worn pipe is usually maintenance and standard homeowners policies often exclude it. Many cover sudden and accidental damage from a burst, but policies vary, so read yours.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle urgent pipe failures at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Repiping is planned, but a burst pipe is not. We send a licensed plumber for the emergency and can plan the larger job afterward. After-hours visits are priced differently, with the price given before work starts.

How do I know if my house has galvanized pipe?

Galvanized pipe is dull gray, threaded at the joints and a magnet sticks to it. Copper is orange-brown, and PEX is red, blue or white plastic. If the pipe is flaking or the water runs rusty, mention it when you call.

Does repiping require a permit?

Rules for each job vary, so ask the Rocky Hill building department what applies to yours. The plumber knows the questions to bring and can tell you what documents the department may want.

Will I be without water the whole time?

Usually not. Water is typically off for part of each day while connections are made. The plumber plans the days with you so cooking, bathing and sleeping stay manageable.

Is PEX as good as copper?

Both are accepted for residential supply when installed correctly. PEX is flexible and faster in finished walls. Copper has a longer track record and handles sunlight and heat well. The plumber recommends one for your layout.
