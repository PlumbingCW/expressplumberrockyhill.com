Emergency Water Line Repair in Rocky Hill, CT

The water service line runs from the street to your house, and when it fails you lose pressure, water or both. Our 24/7 line sends a licensed plumber to find the break.

If water is surfacing in your yard, the meter is spinning with every tap closed or the whole house has lost pressure, the service line may have failed. Our line is open 24/7, and a licensed plumber can often come the same day when schedules allow. Close the main valve inside the house and call us.

The service line is the pipe that carries water from the utility main in the street, through the meter, to your home. Most of it is buried, so a break shows up as a symptom rather than a sight. Finding where it ends and who owns each part comes first.

What to do right now

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Close the main shutoff valve inside the house. If water still runs, the leak is ahead of it, between the street and the valve.
  2. Look at the meter. If it keeps moving with the house valve closed, write down the reading and the time.
  3. Keep people and pets away from soft ground, sinkholes or water pooling near the foundation.
  4. Do not dig. Buried gas, electric and communication lines may be close, and a line break outside is not a reason to skip the safe-digging call.
  5. If water floods the basement near the panel, leave and call 911 before anything else.

Signs the service line has failed

A burst service line rarely looks like a burst pipe. Look for a patch of lawn that stays wet in dry weather, a sunken stretch of driveway or a gurgle near the meter. Inside the house the signs are weak or erratic pressure, discolored water or a meter that never stops.

A total loss of water at every tap is the sharpest sign. Before you assume the line is broken, ask neighbors whether their water is off too. If it is, the cause may be in the street, and the utility is the right call.

Who is responsible for which part of the line

In general the utility maintains the main in the street and the homeowner maintains the pipe from the connection to the house. The exact boundary differs by utility and by property.

How a service line repair goes

01

Confirm the line is the problem

We check the meter, test pressure and rule out leaks inside the house, so you do not pay to dig for a problem that is indoors.

02

Locate the break

Listening gear, a pipe locator and the symptom pattern narrow the break down before any excavation starts.

03

Call Before You Dig

Connecticut law requires excavators using power equipment to call 811 at least two full working days before digging, not counting weekends and holidays. The call is not optional, and forgetting it does not make the job an emergency.

04

Check the street permit

Work inside the town right of way, such as under a street or sidewalk, needs a Street Excavation Permit. Rocky Hill lists it at $50 per location for licensed, insured and bonded contractors.

05

Repair or replace

A short break may be cut out and repaired. A line that has failed more than once is usually better replaced, and we explain both with a price first.

06

Test, restore and clear the line

We pressure test the new work, flush the line and leave the excavation safe. Surface restoration is agreed in advance.

Permits and digging near Rocky Hill streets

The Rocky Hill Engineering Department, at 860-258-2766, handles the Street Excavation Permit for work within town rights of way under Chapter 212 of the town code. An application needs a Call Before You Dig number, and the contractor must be licensed, insured and bonded.

For you, that means a line that crosses the street or sidewalk takes some lead time. A plumber can start with the part on your own property and the permit steps for the rest. For permit questions about the plumbing itself, ask the Rocky Hill building department.

What water line repair costs

We give a clear price before work starts. Digging jobs vary more than most plumbing, so these are the factors.

What moves the price

  • The depth and length of the line and the exact spot of the break.
  • What is above it: lawn is cheaper to reopen than a driveway, walkway or paved road edge.
  • Pipe material and whether the repair or full replacement is chosen.
  • Permits, locating requests and traffic or site protection if the work reaches the street.
  • Time of the call: nights, weekends and holidays are usually priced differently from a weekday visit.
Insurance note: many standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental damage but not the worn part itself. Some offer separate service line coverage, and policies vary, so check yours.

When it may not be the service line

A drop in pressure does not always mean a buried break. A leak inside the house, a stuck regulator or a failed valve causes the same thing. If the problem seems to be indoors, leak detection is the better first step. If a visible pipe inside has split, burst pipe repair covers it.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell us what you see and we book the first plumber who can reach your address. After-hours visits are priced differently from weekday ones, and we give you the price before work starts.

Is a broken water line my responsibility or the utility's?

Usually the utility handles the main in the street and you handle the pipe from the connection to your house. The boundary varies, so ask your water utility where it ends. If the leak is on the street side of the meter, call the utility first.

Do I need a permit to dig up the service line?

Work in the town right of way needs a Street Excavation Permit, and every dig needs a Call Before You Dig call to 811. For the plumbing permit question, ask the Rocky Hill building department. We walk you through which steps apply to your job.

How can I tell if my water meter shows a service line leak?

Shut every tap and appliance, then close your main valve inside the house. Check whether the meter still moves. If it does, water is escaping between the meter and the valve. Write down the reading and tell us when you call.

Can the line be fixed without digging up my whole yard?

Often yes. Locating the break first limits the digging to a small section. The plumber explains whether a spot repair or a longer replacement makes sense and prices each one before work starts.

Do you serve Wethersfield, Newington and Cromwell?

Yes. We serve those towns from Rocky Hill, and the dispatcher confirms availability for your address when you call. Our Wethersfield page explains how we cover the north side.
