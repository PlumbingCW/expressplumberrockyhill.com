Tree Roots in a Sewer Line: Signs, Fixes and Prevention

Roots enter through small gaps, so clearing them without fixing the gap buys only a little time.

By the Rocky Hill Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026.

  • Cable vs jetting

Tree roots grow toward the moisture and nutrients inside a sewer pipe. They slip in through a loose joint or a hairline crack, then thicken into a mat that catches paper and waste. The first signs are slow drains, gurgling and a toilet that bubbles when water runs elsewhere in the house.

A cable or jetter can clear roots, but they return unless the opening is repaired. If sewage is backing up into a tub or floor drain right now, call (860) 397-8103 and stay out of the wet area. Our dispatch line is open 24/7.

Signs roots are in your sewer line

Root problems build slowly and then seem to arrive all at once.

  • Several drains slow down together, especially the lowest ones in the house.
  • Gurgling from a toilet or floor drain when you run the washer or flush.
  • A backup that clears with a snake and returns within weeks or months.
  • Sewage smell in the yard or basement.
  • A greener or faster-growing patch of lawn along the pipe path.
  • A dip or soft spot in the yard above the line.

Sewage on the floor

Raw sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out, do not walk through it barefoot, and wash hands and shoes afterward. Stop using water in the house, since every flush adds to the spill.

Open a window if the smell is strong. If anyone feels ill, call a doctor. Call us at (860) 397-8103 and we will tell you what to do until the plumber arrives.

How roots get in and why they come back

A sewer pipe is a sealed run of pipe segments. Joints can shift as the soil settles, and old pipe can crack. A pinhole is enough. The warm, nutrient-rich water inside is an easy target for a root tip.

Once inside, the root thickens and sends out a mass of fine hairs. Cutting the mass clears the pipe, but the root stays in the gap and regrows. That is why a line cleared in spring can be blocked again in a season or two.

Each method fits a different condition of the pipe. A camera inspection tells you which one applies.

Prevention that actually helps

Keep new trees well away from the sewer line. A large tree needs a much wider berth than a small shrub, and willows, maples and poplars are known for aggressive roots. Ask a local landscaper what suits your yard.

If a line has repeated root trouble, scheduled hydro jetting can keep it open between repairs. Chemical root killers are a short-term patch at best. They do not fix the opening and some can harm the tree. A plumber can advise on what is safe for your pipe.

Mistakes that make root problems worse

  1. Clearing the line again and again without a camera look.
  2. Planting a tree directly over the sewer path.
  3. Pouring chemical cleaner into a backed-up drain, which can splash on whoever opens the line.
  4. Digging in the yard without calling 811 first.
  5. Ignoring early signs such as slow drains and gurgling.
  6. Waiting for sewage on the floor before calling.

Digging, permits and who owns what in Rocky Hill

The Metropolitan District Commission provides sewer service in Rocky Hill. Ask MDC where its responsibility ends and yours begins before you plan a repair, because that decides who handles which part of the pipe.

Work that crosses a street or sidewalk needs a Town Street Excavation Permit under Chapter 212 of the town code. It costs $50 per location, requires a licensed, insured and bonded contractor, and needs a Call Before You Dig number. Connecticut law requires a call at least two full working days before digging, and forgetting to call does not count as an emergency. Call 811.

Questions homeowners ask

How do I know if tree roots are in my sewer line?

Slow drains on several fixtures, gurgling and repeat backups are the common signs. Only a camera confirms roots. If a snake keeps hitting something solid at the same spot, roots or a break are likely.

Can roots break a sewer pipe?

Roots usually enter through an existing gap, but they widen it as they grow. Over time they can crack old pipe and push joints apart. Bellied or collapsed pipe is often the result of ground movement, with roots making it worse.

Is cabling or jetting better for roots?

Cabling cuts roots quickly and is often enough to restore flow. Jetting cleans the pipe wall more fully. Neither one closes the entry point, so a camera look matters if roots return.

Will trenchless lining stop roots?

A properly installed liner seals joints and cracks from the inside, which removes the entry points. It does not suit every pipe, and collapsed or badly offset sections need a different fix. A camera helps decide.

Do I need to call 811 before a sewer repair?

If power equipment will dig, Connecticut law requires a call at least two full working days before digging. Call 811. Skipping the call does not qualify as an emergency excavation.

Who is responsible for the sewer pipe in Rocky Hill?

MDC provides sewer service in Rocky Hill, so ask MDC where its responsibility stops. Work across the street needs a town excavation permit.
