Leaky Outdoor Faucet Repair in Rocky Hill, CT

A dripping spigot is usually a worn washer or packing nut, but a leak inside the wall after a freeze is a different job.

A leaky outdoor faucet is most often a worn washer, a loose packing nut or a cracked valve body. If water drips from the spout when the handle is closed, the washer is worn. If it seeps around the handle stem, the packing is the problem. Both are small repairs. Water showing up inside the house, in a basement ceiling or on a wall, is not.

Outdoor faucets are also the first thing to fail when a Connecticut cold snap arrives. A hose left attached traps water in the faucet, and that water can freeze and split the pipe behind the wall. This guide covers the causes, what you can safely do yourself, how to winterize, and when to call a licensed plumber.

Where the water is coming from tells you the fix

Look closely before you buy parts. Run a hand along the faucet with the handle fully closed and the hose off. The wet spot points to the failed part.

A simple, older hose bib has a rubber washer that presses on a seat. Over the years the washer hardens and the seat roughens. A frost-free sillcock is built differently, with a long stem that places the shutoff seat well inside the heated part of the house. That design is smarter for winter, but it fails in its own ways.

Drip from the spout

The washer or the seat is worn. On a basic hose bib, replacing the washer often stops it. On a frost-free sillcock, the stem and washer assembly is replaced as a unit, and that is easier with the water shut off inside.

Weeping around the handle

The packing nut or packing washer has loosened or dried out. A quarter turn on the nut sometimes stops it. If tightening does nothing, the packing needs replacing.

Water inside the wall or from the basement ceiling

This usually means the pipe behind the faucet cracked or the sillcock body split. Shut off the water to that line and call. Running the hose will only push water into the wall.

Hose bib or frost-free sillcock: how each one leaks

Many homes have one of each, so it helps to know which you have. A frost-free sillcock has a long body that slopes slightly downward and sticks out from the wall.

Any faucet that drips only when a hose is attached has a different problem than one that drips on its own.

What to do right now

Most outdoor faucets have their own shutoff valve inside the house, often in the basement or crawl space, close to the wall where the faucet sits.

Stop a leaking outdoor faucet safely

  1. Disconnect the hose and any splitter or timer so water has somewhere to drain.
  2. Find the interior shutoff for that faucet and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  3. Open the outdoor faucet to let the pipe drain, then leave the handle open.
  4. If there is no interior shutoff, shut the main valve and call us.
  5. Check the wall, ceiling and floor near the pipe for dampness. Wet drywall or a stained joist means the leak is inside.
  6. Do not use a torch or a heat gun on a frozen pipe. Open nearby taps and call.

How a frozen pipe turns a drip into a leak

Water expands as it freezes. When a hose stays on the faucet through a cold night, the water between the hose and the valve freezes first. The ice pushes back into the pipe and can split it, usually a few inches inside the wall.

The split often hides until the weather warms. The ice melts, the pipe leaks, and you see water only when the faucet is used again in spring, or when the ceiling below begins to stain. A leak found early is a pipe repair. A leak found late can mean wet insulation, drywall and mold.

Signs the pipe behind the faucet is damaged

Water sprays from the wall side of the faucet when it is turned on. The faucet hisses with the handle closed. The water meter keeps moving when every fixture is off. Any of these is a reason to shut the line off and call, because a split pipe can release a lot of water quickly.

Winterize outdoor faucets before the first hard freeze

01

Remove every hose

Take off hoses, splitters, nozzles and timers. A hose left attached is the most common cause of a split pipe.

02

Shut off the interior valve

If the faucet has its own shutoff inside, close it. This takes pressure off the line during the coldest weeks.

03

Drain the faucet

Open the outdoor handle so the water between the shutoff and the spout runs out, then leave it open until spring.

04

Add a cover if you like

An insulated cover helps a basic hose bib, but it is not a substitute for draining the line.

05

Check frost-free sillcocks for slope

A frost-free sillcock only drains if the body tilts slightly downward. If it points level or upward, water can sit inside and freeze anyway.

Mistakes that cost more later

Common mistakes with outdoor faucets

  • Leaving the hose on through the first cold nights.
  • Overtightening the packing nut until the handle will not turn.
  • Using thread seal tape on the wrong threads and adding stress to an old pipe.
  • Ignoring a slow drip that has already worn the seat.
  • Assuming a frost-free sillcock cannot freeze.
  • Forcing a stuck handle with pliers, which can twist the pipe inside the wall.

When to repair and when to replace

A washer or packing repair is cheap and quick. Replacement makes sense when the body is cracked, the threads are stripped, the handle is stuck or the faucet is more than a few years past its best. A new frost-free sillcock also lets you fix a poor slope and add a vacuum breaker.

Replacing a faucet often means working at the pipe inside the wall, sometimes with soldering or a push-fit connection. If that is outside what you are comfortable with, call. Our faucet repair service covers outdoor faucets as well as sinks.

Water coming through the wall or ceiling?

Shut off the water to the outdoor line or the main valve, turn off power to any wet area if you can do so safely, and call. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 for an electrical hazard.

Questions homeowners ask

Why does my outdoor faucet drip when it is turned off?

The washer inside has worn or the seat is rough. On an older hose bib, replacing the washer often fixes it. On a frost-free sillcock, the stem assembly is replaced. If it still drips afterward, the valve body is probably worn and the faucet should be replaced.

Can a frost-free faucet still freeze?

Yes. A frost-free sillcock puts the shutoff seat inside the heated space, but it only drains if the body slopes down and the hose is removed. A hose left on or a level installation can hold water that freezes and splits the pipe.

How do I know if the pipe inside the wall split?

Look for spray from the wall side of the faucet, a hiss with the handle closed, damp drywall, ceiling stains below, or a water meter that moves with everything off. If you see any of these, shut off the line and call a plumber.

Can I replace an outdoor faucet myself?

A simple threaded hose bib can be a DIY job if the shutoff works and you can reach the pipe. Soldered or inaccessible connections, stuck faucets and any sign of a split pipe are better left to a licensed plumber.

Do I need to shut off the water to the faucet in winter?

It is a good habit. Remove the hose, close the interior shutoff if there is one, and open the outdoor handle to drain the line. A faucet with no interior shutoff should still have its hose removed before the first hard freeze.
