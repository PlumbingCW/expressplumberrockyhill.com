Install or Replace a Toilet Flange in Rocky Hill, CT

A damaged flange lets the toilet rock, leak at the base and release sewer gas, and it is not always a quick swap.

By the Rocky Hill Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026.

  • Rocking toilet
  • Wax ring leaks
  • Closet bolts

The toilet flange is the ring that bolts the toilet to the floor and connects it to the drain pipe. If it is cracked, rusted, loose or set too low, the toilet will rock, leak at the base or smell. A new wax ring alone will not fix a broken flange. The flange is repaired or replaced, and the wax ring and closet bolts are renewed at the same time.

This guide explains how to tell if the flange is the problem, what the repair involves, how the height should sit against the finished floor, and when floor damage makes it a job for a licensed plumber.

What the flange does and how it fails

The flange screws or bolts to the floor and sits on top of the drain pipe. The wax ring sits between the flange and the toilet outlet and seals water and gas. Two closet bolts hold the toilet down.

Flanges fail from age and movement. Cast iron rusts and breaks around the slots. Plastic cracks. Brass bends. Many are damaged when the toilet was rocked loose for years, or when a thick new floor was laid and the flange was not raised.

Signs the flange is the problem

Toilet rocks or shifts

Tightening the bolts does not help or the bolts will not hold. The flange may be broken, loose on the floor or the bolts may be spinning in the slots.

Water around the base

A wet floor after flushing points to a failed wax seal. If a new ring fails again soon, the flange height or position is usually wrong.

Sewer smell

A gap between the flange and the toilet lets gas escape. The smell is stronger after flushing.

Soft or stained floor

Slow leaks rot the subfloor around the flange. Pressing on the floor near the toilet and feeling it give is a warning sign.

Flange types and repair options

The right fix depends on the material, the damage and the condition of the floor under it.

Always ask how the repair will be anchored. A flange needs solid material to screw into.

Flange height and why it matters

The top of the flange should sit on or very near the finished floor. If it sits well below the floor, a standard wax ring cannot compress enough to seal. If it sits far above, the toilet cannot sit flat.

New flooring is a common reason for a bad seal. Tile or thick vinyl can lift the finished floor above the old flange. A spacer ring or extension kit brings the sealing surface back up. These parts vary by product, so ask what height your flange needs for the toilet you have.

How a flange and wax ring replacement goes

01

Shut off and empty the toilet

Close the supply valve, flush, hold the handle and sponge out remaining water. Disconnect the supply line.

02

Lift the toilet off

Remove the closet bolt nuts and lift straight up. Toilets are heavy, so use help. Set it on a towel.

03

Remove the old wax and flange

Scrape off old wax. Inspect the flange, the pipe and the floor. Unscrew or cut out a damaged flange.

04

Repair the floor if needed

Soft or stained wood is cut back and replaced so the new flange has solid material to bolt into.

05

Set the new flange and ring

Anchor the flange to the floor, install new closet bolts, set a new wax ring and lower the toilet straight down.

06

Tighten, connect and test

Tighten the nuts evenly, reconnect water and flush several times while checking the base.

Mistakes that cause a second leak

Common flange and wax ring mistakes

  • Adding extra wax rings instead of fixing the flange height.
  • Overtightening the closet bolts and cracking the base.
  • Reusing a wax ring after lifting the toilet.
  • Screwing a new flange into rotted wood.
  • Using the wrong size bolts or mismatched washers.
  • Setting the toilet down and sliding it, which breaks the seal.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call if the flange is cast iron and broken, if the drain pipe below it is damaged, if the floor is soft or stained, or if you cannot lift the toilet safely. A drain that sits below the flange, or leaks onto a ceiling below, is also a job for a plumber.

Questions homeowners ask

How do I know if my toilet flange is broken?

The toilet rocks, the bolts will not hold, you see rust or cracks on the ring, or a new wax ring leaks again. Lifting the toilet shows the flange. A broken or bent ring needs repair or replacement.

Can I replace a wax ring without replacing the flange?

Yes, if the flange is solid, firmly anchored and at the right height. If it is cracked, loose, rusted or too low, a new ring will leak again. Inspect the flange every time the toilet comes up.

How high should a toilet flange sit?

The flange should sit at or very close to the finished floor so the wax ring can seal. Flanges set well below the floor often need a spacer or extension kit. Ask what your toilet and floor require.

What if the subfloor around the toilet is soft?

A flange cannot hold in soft wood. The rotted section must be cut out and replaced before a new flange is installed. A plumber can find how far the damage runs and repair it.

Why does my toilet smell of sewer gas?

A gap in the wax seal or a broken flange can let gas into the room. The smell is often worse after flushing. Replace the wax ring and check the flange. A smell that persists may point to a vent or trap issue.
