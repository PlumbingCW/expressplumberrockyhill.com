Install a Garbage Disposal on a Double Sink in Rocky Hill, CT

A double sink adds one extra drain connection, and the power supply is the part most people should not guess about.

By the Rocky Hill Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026.

On a double sink, the disposal mounts under one bowl. The second bowl drains through a tailpiece into a continuous waste arm, and both flow to one trap. The dishwasher drain connects to a port on the disposal after the knockout plug is removed. Power must be off at the breaker for the entire job.

This guide covers how the plumbing fits together, what changes with a double bowl, the dishwasher knockout, and where plumbing ends and electrical work begins. If wiring is needed, a licensed electrician should do it.

How a double bowl sink drains with a disposal

A double sink has two drain openings. The disposal takes one. The other bowl needs a tailpiece that runs to the disposal's side outlet or to a waste arm that joins the disposal discharge before the trap.

Everything then drains through a single trap and into the wall. The plan matters because a poor arrangement leaves standing water in the second bowl or causes gurgling.

Most kitchens can be set up with a disposal waste elbow, a continuous waste arm and a P-trap. Which parts fit depends on the sink depth, cabinet space and the location of the wall drain.

Plumbing steps for the install

01

Turn off power and water

Switch off the breaker for the sink area and, if replacing, unplug or disconnect the old unit. Close the shutoff valves under the sink.

02

Remove the old drain parts

Take off the trap and tailpieces, and remove the old sink flange from bowl one.

03

Seat the new sink flange

Set the flange with plumber's putty or its gasket, attach the mounting ring and tighten evenly.

04

Remove the dishwasher knockout if needed

If a dishwasher connects, knock out the plug inside the dishwasher inlet with a hammer and screwdriver. Remove the loose plug from the unit.

05

Mount the disposal and connect the waste arm

Lock the disposal to the mount, align the outlet, attach the waste elbow and the second bowl tailpiece to the continuous waste arm.

06

Connect the dishwasher hose and the trap

Clamp the dishwasher drain hose to the inlet with a high loop. Join the arm to the P-trap.

07

Test for leaks

Restore water, fill both bowls, release them and check every joint with a dry towel before restoring power.

Dishwasher knockout: do this once

Most new disposals have a plastic plug in the dishwasher inlet. If a dishwasher is connected and the plug stays in, the dishwasher cannot drain. Knock it out, then fish the loose plug from the disposal chamber so it does not jam the unit.

Electrical safety and who should wire it

A disposal needs power from a switch or an outlet under the sink. Switch off the circuit at the breaker and confirm it is dead before touching any wire. Water and electricity are a dangerous mix.

If there is no outlet or switch, or the wiring looks damaged, stop. New wiring is electrical work, not plumbing, and a licensed electrician should handle it. Never run a disposal with a missing cover or an exposed wire. If you suspect a shock hazard, leave the area and call 911.

Cord or hardwire

Some disposals come with a cord and plug and others are hardwired. A cord needs a grounded outlet in reach. Hardwiring should be done by a licensed electrician. Ask the Rocky Hill building department what applies.

Common disposal install mistakes

Mistakes that cause leaks and clogs

  • Overtightening the mounting ring and cracking a stainless sink.
  • Forgetting to remove the dishwasher knockout.
  • Leaving the second bowl without enough slope to drain.
  • Setting the trap too high or too low for the wall stub.
  • Letting the dishwasher hose run straight down with no high loop.
  • Running the unit before checking leaks and power.

When to call a plumber

Call if the drain parts do not line up, the wall drain is the wrong height, you need a new outlet, or the sink is stone or composite and hard to work around. Also call if you smell gas, see water near the electrical connection, or the unit has jammed or leaks.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I install a garbage disposal on a double sink?

Yes. The disposal mounts under one bowl, and the second bowl drains through a tailpiece into a continuous waste arm that joins the disposal outlet before the P-trap. Cabinet space and the wall drain height decide the layout.

What is the dishwasher knockout?

It is a plastic plug in the disposal's dishwasher inlet. If a dishwasher drains into the unit, the plug must be knocked out. Remove the loose plug from inside the chamber afterward.

Do I need an electrician for a garbage disposal?

If there is a working outlet or switch under the sink, a cord-connected unit may plug in. New wiring, a new circuit or hardwiring should be done by a licensed electrician. Always turn off the breaker first.

Why does the second bowl drain slowly after install?

The waste arm may lack slope, the tailpiece may be too long or the trap may be too high. A trap that is set too low or a partial clog can do the same. A plumber can reset the arrangement.

Can a disposal go on any sink?

Most standard kitchen sinks with a three-bolt or similar drain opening work. Stone, composite and thin stainless sinks need extra care. Check the sink maker's instructions.
