Water-Powered Sump Pumps: How They Work and When They Fit

A water-powered pump keeps pumping when the power is out, as long as your home still has water pressure.

By the Rocky Hill Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026.

A water-powered sump pump is a backup unit that uses the pressure of your home's water supply to push water out of the sump basin. It has no motor and needs no electricity. When the main pump stops, the float opens a valve, supply water rushes through the unit and the jet pulls basement water out with it.

It works well when the power goes out during a storm, but only if water pressure holds, and it uses a lot of water while it runs. Sump pump stopped and water rising? Call (860) 397-8103 and our 24/7 line will send a licensed plumber.

How a water-powered backup pump works

01

It connects to a cold water line

A supply pipe runs from your house plumbing to the unit, which hangs or sits in the sump basin beside the main pump. A shutoff valve and a backflow protection device belong on that supply.

02

A float triggers it

If the water level climbs above the point where the main pump should have started, a float opens the supply valve. The backup stays idle until that happens.

03

A jet creates suction

Supply water squeezes through a narrow nozzle at high speed. That fast stream creates suction, the same effect as a venturi in a garden sprayer.

04

Basement water leaves with the supply water

The suction draws sump water into the stream. Both flow out through the discharge pipe together.

05

It shuts off when the level drops

As the float falls, the supply valve closes and the unit stops using water.

The catch: it needs steady municipal pressure

These pumps depend on the water pressure at your house. Every model lists a minimum supply pressure, and output drops as pressure drops. Check the manufacturer's data sheet for the model you are considering.

Rocky Hill homes are served by the Metropolitan District Commission, which delivers water mostly by gravity from its reservoirs. That means the supply does not rely on household power the way a private well pump does. It does not mean pressure is the same at every house, so test yours with a gauge on an outside spigot before you buy.

A home on a private well will not suit this design, because well pressure comes from an electric pump that stops during an outage.

Water-powered backup compared with battery backup

Both are backups to a main electric pump. They fail in different ways, so the better choice depends on your house.

Neither type replaces a good main pump. Both are insurance for the time the main pump cannot run.

What to know about water use

Water use is the most common surprise with this type of pump.

It uses more than it moves

Many models use more supply water than the volume they pump out. The ratio depends on pressure and on how high the water must be lifted. Check the manufacturer's figures.

You pay for the water

Water is billed to MDC ratepayers in Rocky Hill, so a long storm with the backup running adds to the bill. Ask MDC about rates, since we do not state utility charges.

The discharge handles both flows

The pipe carries your basement water and the supply water together. It needs to be sized and routed to handle the combined flow.

It is a backup, not a daily pump

Running one for hours every rainy week is hard on your bill. If your main pump fails often, the fix is a better main pump.

When a water-powered pump makes sense

  • Your power goes out often during storms and you want a backup with no battery to maintain.
  • Your supply pressure is steady and meets the pump's published minimum.
  • You are on municipal water, not a private well.
  • Your main pump is in good shape and the backup would run only rarely.
  • The discharge pipe can carry the extra flow away from the house.

Check local rules before it goes in

Connecting a water supply to a device that can sit in dirty basin water raises backflow and cross-connection questions. Required protection and any permits are set locally, so we do not state them here.

Ask the Rocky Hill building department what applies to your job, and ask MDC whether it has requirements for this connection. A licensed plumber should install the supply side. Our sump pump service covers backups, basins and discharge piping.

Mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying one without testing your water pressure first.
  2. Installing it as the main pump instead of a backup.
  3. Skipping a yearly test, so a stuck valve or float is found during a storm.
  4. Leaving the discharge pipe undersized for the combined flow.
  5. Assuming the backup will keep up with a failed main pump during a long storm.
  6. Doing the supply connection without checking backflow and permit rules with the town.

Questions homeowners ask

Do water-powered sump pumps work during a power outage?

Yes, because they use water pressure instead of electricity. They keep working as long as your supply pressure holds at the level the pump needs. If pressure drops, so does the pump's output.

Will a water-powered pump raise my water bill?

It can. Many models use more supply water than they remove, and water is billed to MDC ratepayers in Rocky Hill. A short outage costs little. A long storm with constant running costs more.

Is a water-powered pump better than a battery backup?

Neither is better for every house. Water-powered units never run down, but they use water and need steady pressure. Battery units use no water but need battery care. Your pressure and outage history decide it.

Can I use one if I have a private well?

Usually not. Well pressure comes from an electric pump that stops when the power goes out. That is the moment you need the backup. Battery backup is the usual choice there.

Do I need a permit to install a water-powered backup pump?

We cannot say for your address. Permit and backflow requirements are set locally. Ask the Rocky Hill building department, and ask MDC about any requirement for the water connection.

Can a water-powered pump replace my main sump pump?

It is meant as a backup. It uses too much water for daily duty. If your main pump keeps failing, replace it. We can check the float, the check valve and the discharge line during a visit.
