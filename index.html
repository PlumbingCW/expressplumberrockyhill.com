What counts as a plumbing emergency

A plumbing emergency is any problem that is damaging your home right now or makes it unsafe to use. Active flooding, a burst or split pipe, sewage coming up through a drain, and no water at all are the clear cases. A water heater leaking onto the floor belongs on the list too.

Some problems can wait for a same-day appointment. A slow drain, a dripping faucet or a toilet that runs after flushing is annoying but not dangerous. If you are unsure which group yours falls into, call and describe it. The dispatcher will tell you honestly whether it needs a plumber tonight.

Do these three things first

Find the main shutoff valve and close it. It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in the basement or utility room. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If water is coming from a single fixture, close the small valve under the sink or behind the toilet instead.

Open a faucet at the lowest level of the house to drain the pipes and relieve pressure. Then move valuables and rugs out of the wet area and call us at (860) 397-8103.

When to leave the building

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency number from outside. Do not switch lights on or off. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay clear and shut power off only if you can do it from a dry spot. Sewage on the floor is also a reason to keep children and pets away.

Why homeowners call this line

You get a licensed plumber, not a call center that hands your job to whoever answers. Every job is done by licensed plumbers, and the company is licensed and insured. Connecticut issues plumbing licenses through the Department of Consumer Protection, and you can check any plumber's license class with them before work starts.

Plumbers work from model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Connecticut's state building code is based on the 2021 International Codes. For bigger jobs like a water heater swap, ask the town building department what applies.

You also hear the price first. Once the plumber has seen the problem, you get a clear price before work starts, and the work begins only when you say yes. Cost depends on the cause, the access, the parts and the hour, so we explain what drives the number rather than quoting blind over the phone.

When the schedule allows, we offer same-day appointments, and the line is open 24/7 for the urgent ones. We serve Rocky Hill and nearby towns from the office on Silas Deane Highway.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Describe the problem and we book the first plumber who can come to your address. After-hours visits are priced differently from weekday ones, and you get the price before work starts.

What should I do first when a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve, then open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the pipes. Move belongings away from the water and call. If the water is near outlets or the electrical panel, keep clear and tell the dispatcher.

Do you cover every ZIP code and nearby town?

Our home base is Rocky Hill, ZIP 06067, and we serve Wethersfield, Newington, Cromwell and other nearby towns. Coverage depends on the day and the schedule, so call with your address and we tell you whether we can help.

How much will it cost?

It depends on the cause, the access, the parts and the time of day. You get a clear price before work starts, and the work begins only after you agree. We do not quote a fixed number over the phone for a problem nobody has seen.

Can you come the same day?

We offer same-day appointments when available. Emergencies are handled first, and routine jobs fill the remaining openings. Call early in the day if the problem can wait a few hours.

Are your plumbers licensed and insured?

Yes. The work is done by licensed plumbers, and the company is licensed and insured. You can check any Connecticut plumber's license class with the Department of Consumer Protection before work starts.

Is the water leak in the street or inside my house?

If water is coming up in the street or at the meter, that is on the utility side and MDC handles it. If the leak is past the meter, it is yours, and we can help. Not sure which one it is? Call and we sort it out with you.
