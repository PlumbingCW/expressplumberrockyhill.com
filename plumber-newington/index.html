How a 1950s ranch differs from a 1970s split

Newington grew fast after the war. Its population rose from 5,449 in 1940 to 9,110 in 1950. The housing data shows 20 percent of units built in the 1950s, 17 percent in the 1960s and 23 percent in the 1970s. About 53 percent predate 1970.

For you, the decade matters because it hints at what is behind the drywall. A house from the 1950s may have older drain lines and a basement with a floor drain. A 1970s house is more likely to have a finished lower level, which raises the stakes when water arrives. Tell us the age of the house when you call.

Older houses can also run into today's rules. Plumbers follow model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Connecticut's state building code is based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the building department what applies before a remodel.

MDC sewer and water: where the utility ends

Newington is an MDC member town for both water and sewer. The public sewer main under the street is the utility's. The pipe that runs from your house to the connection is generally your responsibility.

If neighbors on your block are all backing up at once, call MDC at (860) 278-7850 about the public main. If it is only your house, the blockage is probably on your side and we can clear it.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

Do these while you wait for the plumber.

1

Shut the water

Close the main valve or the fixture shutoff. Open one faucet at the lowest level to relieve pressure.

2

Cut power if safe

If water reaches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911. Do not touch electrical gear while standing in water.

3

Protect the lower level

Many Newington homes have finished basements. Lift furniture and electronics off the floor.

4

Say what failed

Tell the dispatcher the fixture, the age of the house and whether you smell sewage.
