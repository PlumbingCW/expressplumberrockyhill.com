Commercial plumbing is not residential work at a larger scale. Pipes are bigger, fixtures serve many people, and a failure can close a business for the day. We work in shops, offices, restaurants and light industrial buildings, and we aim to make the repair as quickly as safe work allows.

When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Some problems can wait until morning and some cannot. A single slow sink is a daytime job. A burst line, sewage coming up through a floor drain, a restroom with no water or an overflowing grease trap needs a plumber now.

Health rules often require working restrooms and handwashing. Food businesses depend on hot water and clean drains to stay open. For you, that means a plumbing failure can be a business problem, not only a repair bill. Call early and tell us what is closed.

What to do right now

  1. Find and close the shutoff for the affected area, or the main shutoff if you cannot tell which line is leaking.
  2. Move stock, paper records and electronics off the floor and away from the water.
  3. Do not use drains or toilets that are backing up. Block off the area if sewage is present.
  4. If water is near panels, outlets or machinery, stay clear and have the power shut off by a qualified person. Call 911 if anyone is at risk.
  5. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency number from outside.
  6. Call us and tell us the type of business, the fixture or line affected and whether the building can stay open.

Restaurant and kitchen plumbing

Kitchens put more load on plumbing than any other business. Fat, oil and food solids go down the drain all day and harden further along the line. A line that was slow on Friday can be fully blocked by Saturday dinner.

For repeat blockages, a cable machine may open the line and hydro jetting can scrub the pipe wall. Our hydro jetting page explains when that is the better method. If the line keeps backing up in the same place, a camera can show a break or a belly in the pipe.

After-hours and weekend service

Many commercial failures happen overnight, when no one is there to see a burst line. A manager arriving Monday morning to a flooded floor is a common call. If you have an alarm company or a key holder, give them our number so they know whom to call.

After-hours visits are priced differently from weekday ones. You get the price before work starts. We can often make a temporary fix first to stop the water, and then schedule the full repair for business hours so the building is usable.

Planned work that avoids emergencies

Many emergencies start as small problems. A weeping valve, a rusty shutoff or a water heater that cycles all day will fail eventually. A walk through the building with a plumber can find these before they close you down.

We also handle remodels, tenant improvements and fixture installs. If you are adding restrooms or moving a sink line, see our plumbing installation page. Ask the Rocky Hill building department what permits apply to your project before work begins.

What commercial plumbing costs

We give a clear price before work starts. Commercial jobs vary more than residential ones, so these are the main factors.

What moves the price

  • The size and type of the line, and how many fixtures it serves.
  • Access: ceiling spaces, slab floors and mechanical rooms take longer to reach.
  • Equipment needed: a jetter, a camera or a larger cable machine adds cost.
  • Whether the work must happen outside business hours to keep you open.
  • Parts and materials, including commercial-grade fixtures and valves.
  • Time of the call: nights, weekends and holidays are priced differently.
Insurance note: a business property policy may cover sudden and accidental water damage and, in some cases, lost income. It usually does not cover gradual leaks or the worn part itself. Policies vary, so check yours or ask your agent.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Rocky Hill?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell us the business type and what has failed, and we book the first plumber who can reach you. After-hours visits are priced differently, and we give the price before work starts.

What kinds of buildings do you service?

We work in shops, offices, restaurants and light industrial buildings. For very large systems, specialized process equipment or boiler plants, the dispatcher will tell you honestly whether we are the right fit when you call.

Can you make a temporary fix so we can reopen?

Often yes. Stopping a leak, capping a line or clearing a drain can get a business open while a full repair is scheduled. The plumber explains what is safe to use and what is not, and gives the price for each step before starting.

Who pays when a drain backs up, me or the landlord?

That depends on your lease and where the blockage sits. A clog in a fixture or branch line is often the tenant's concern, while a building main may be the owner's. Check your lease. We can tell you where the problem is.

Do you clear grease traps and kitchen drains?

We clear kitchen drain lines and can diagnose why a line backs up. Routine grease trap pumping is a separate service done by haulers. If a trap overflows or its outlet line is blocked, call us for the plumbing side.

Do you service Wethersfield, Newington and Cromwell businesses?

Yes. We serve those towns from Rocky Hill, and the dispatcher confirms availability for your address when you call. Our Newington page covers the Berlin Turnpike side, and our Wethersfield page covers the north.
